### Title
FORWARDED (or JUST BETWEEN US?)

### Authors
aya maurice rai

### One-liner
A narrative game from the perspective of classmates using a chat application, where a private joke becomes public humiliation when a trusted friend forwards it and the others keep it circulating.

### Pitch
**FORWARDED** is a short narrative game set in a Snapchat-inspired chat application, where a teenager witnesses a private, funny video being shared beyond its intended audience, **turning a moment of trust into public humiliation.** Through **the choices they make in messages and reactions**, the player experiences how everyday digital interactions can contribute to collective cyberbullying.
⟶ snapchat inspired : the flames to represetn directly closeness through the interface

### Prevention
- The game aims to raise awareness of how sharing content outside its original context can transform a harmless private moment into a harmful public experience.
- By experiencing how a video moves between conversations and audiences, players are encouraged to reflect on trust, consent, and the importance of context when sharing someone else's content.
- The important distinction is that Camille's video was funny even to Camille herself. The problem wasn't the content, but how, where, and with whom it was shared.

- Through the player's everyday actions as a classmate, the game also explores the role of bystanders in collective cyberbullying. Seemingly insignificant choices, such as reacting, commenting, forwarding, or remaining silent, reveal how the player's own behaviour can contribute to the escalation of a situation. The game encourages players to reflect on their responsibility and the difficulty of intervening when friends are involved, without explicitly presenting choices as right or wrong.

### Story & Setting
- The story takes place within a group of secondary-school classmates, aged around 14, whose social lives unfold through a Snapchat-inspired messaging application.
- One Friday evening, Léa shares a funny karaoke video of her friend Camille in a group chat. Camille had originally sent the video privately to Léa, trusting her not to share it. What starts as a harmless joke gradually spreads beyond the original conversation, as classmates transform the video into memes, stickers, and screenshots shared across different social circles.
- The player is part of the friend group chat and has a friendly but not particularly close relationship with Camille. As the video reaches a wider audience and Camille asks people to stop sharing it, the player finds themselves caught between their closer friendships within the group and their concern for Camille. The story unfolds over a Friday evening and the following Monday, ending with a shift to Camille's perspective, revealing how she experienced the same events.

### Core Mechanics
#### Interactions
- The game takes place entirely within a **(Snapchat-inspired) messaging application**, where players navigate group chats and private conversations. Through predefined messages, emoji reactions, forwarding content, or choosing not to respond, they influence how the situation unfolds and how their relationships with their classmates evolve. Players can freely switch between conversations, just as they would in a real messaging application.
#### Social pressure
- Contextual notifications interrupt conversations at key moments, creating competing social pressures and encouraging players to navigate several situations simultaneously. These notifications always come from other characters, while content from external platforms appears through screenshots, videos, or text. Snapchat-inspired friendship streaks (🔥) reinforce the player's existing social relationships.
#### Consequences
- The story follows a fixed narrative, but the player's choices influence its consequences and final reveal. At the end, players revisit the events from Camille's perspective, seeing how their actions contributed to the situation, without being given a morality score or explicitly correct answers.
#### Design consideration
- Allowing players to freely switch between conversations could become overwhelming, especially when several notifications arrive simultaneously. The interface and pacing should therefore balance realistic social pressure with clarity.


### Unique Bits
- A game entirely within a chat interface: Inspired by *Bury Me, My Love*, the entire narrative unfolds through messages, notifications, and shared media, without leaving the fictional messaging application.
- A childlike and caring visual identity: Inspired by *Samuel* by Émilie Tronche, the game adopts a simple, hand-drawn, and expressive visual style, creating a warm and intimate atmosphere that contrasts with the seriousness of the subject.
- A final perspective switch: At the end of the game, players enter Camille's phone, discovering how she experienced the same events and seeing their own actions from the other side of the conversation.
