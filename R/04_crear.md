## 26-11-10
### Pitch
In a secondary school class, an anonymous TikTok account starts posting AI-generated videos featuring students. Laughter quickly turns into suffering, triggering an investigation by a small group of students.

### What is the experience about ? 
#### What is the dilemma of the main characters ?
As users and witnesses, we have the power to act. By liking, sharing, or contributing humiliating content, we “feed the troll,” helping sustain its influence and the harm it causes. Refusing to circulate that content, reporting abuse, and supporting the person targeted can help interrupt this cycle. (la fille qui a directement effacé la photo et contacté l'aggresseur et la victime pour la mettre au courant #example)
⟶ how can we behave collectively to prevent the account from harming
#### How does this dilemma relate to cyberbullying ?
Group dynamics can turn everyday moments into harassment: a sticker can become a weapon, and a picture can lead to someone’s social exclusion. Through peer pressure, conformity, and social reinforcement, a group can normalise cruelty until taking part feels easier than speaking out.
#### How does the "cyber" component affect this dilemma ? 
1. Behind a screen, we often cannot see the reactions of the people we target or discuss. Without their facial expressions or other immediate signs of distress, it can be easier to overlook the harm we cause.
2. Social media can amplify this harm: images can be taken out of context and shared without consent, while recommendation algorithms can extend their reach beyond the original audience.
3. By prioritising engagement over users’ well-being, these systems risk amplifying disturbing or harmful content that holds attention, rather than favouring positive or supportive interactions.
### Agency
#### How does this story explore adolescent agency ?
The story explores adolescents’ ability to influence a group, even when they cannot control the account itself. The characters must decide whether to join in, stay silent, or challenge their friends at the risk of becoming targets themselves. Their agency lies in everyday choices—what they share, what they refuse to contribute, and whom they support—and in their ability to act together (by creating a support system for eachother?) to stop feeding the account.

### Take-away points
#### What tools teenagers can learn from your project
1. Recognise when humour becomes humiliation, even when everyone else is laughing.
2. Interrupt the spread by refusing to like, share, or contribute harmful content.
3. Support the person targeted by checking in privately and asking what help they want.
4. Act together by challenging harmful behaviour with friends, reporting abuse, and seeking support from a trusted adult.

#### How does the story internally lead to that understanding 
The students begin by searching for one person to blame. As their investigation progresses, they discover how their own reactions, submissions, and betrayals sustain the account. Finding its creator is no longer enough: they must change how they participate. By showing the consequences of both feeding the account and withdrawing their support, the story makes collective responsibility tangible.

## 26-11-01_sweet dreams
### Pitch
The game explores the bystander’s actions and choices, limited by societal pressure, and how these get amplified by their usage as cyberharassment. <br>
It unfolds as a linear interactive narrative structured around a day-and-night cycle. 

### Ideas (propose a subtitle more subtle, maybe like Game)
#### Position (sometihng more like the beginings? where the thoguht starts ? maybe the context)
- A bystander is never neutral in a situation of harassment, regardless of whether they choose to intervene or remain passive.

#### Mechanics
- Although the player seems to have a choice – the options to help first generate an error, then disapear, forcing them to contribute to the bullying.

- The “Night/Dream” phases reflects the psyche of the character the player embodies, shaped by their actions and decisions throughout the day.
#### Narration 
- During the day, the player must decide whether or not to intervene in the situations presented to them.

- At first, the player gets rewarded for following and supporting the bully, but after two days, nightmares start coming, trapping the character in mirrors of their daily actions.
#### Conclusion
- When finishing or quitting the game, a message saying "In this game you didn't really have the choice, but in real life you do."
- Quotes of positive stories of support against bullying scrolls.
